# Rush Racers

Race three rivals to the finish across worlds that each move differently: swim a reef in Reef, run and jump through a forest in Thornwood, drift on thrusters through an asteroid belt in Farside. Grab items, hold up to three, and fire the one you pick. A world is a medium, a cast of eight on shared ability slots, the names and art of the shared items, pickups and enemies, and its courses (see "Worlds" below, and "Add a world" in `COURSES.md`).

This is the repo's first full game and its reference layout: logic in feature folders under `src/logic/`, one orchestrating `world.ts`, engine seams (`art/index.ts`, `art/font.ts`, `audio/index.ts`, `themes/index.ts`) that later work swaps behind.

## Courses and themes

A course is data in a registry: `logic/courses/<id>.ts` holds its level, the world it is raced in (which gives its medium), its theme, its race song, whether it is listed on course select, its par time and any tuning, and `logic/courses/index.ts` (`COURSES`, `LISTED_COURSES`, `DEFAULT_COURSE`, `courseById`) is the only file that imports a course file. Three courses are listed, in this order on course select: Sunlit Shallows (Reef), Bramble Run (Thornwood) and Slingshot Alley (Farside). Drift Test (Farside, unlisted) is the small level the drift scenarios race on. Run Test (Thornwood, unlisted) is the run medium's test course. How to add a course, a medium or a world is in `COURSES.md`.

A theme is how a course looks, in the engine: `engine/themes/<id>.ts` built with `defineTheme` (types in `themes/types.ts`) registers its own textures and animations at boot and says how the views draw it: terrain tiles per cell and per legend glyph art, flowing cells (kelp, currents), the finish gate and debris chunk, backdrop bands and painted layers, caustics, light rays and motes (each optional), particle physics (how things rise, fall, drift and splash), hazard skins, the dark-zone tint, the contrast bands sprites and text must stand off, and any extra race layers. `THEMES` and `themeFor(course)` are in `themes/index.ts`. The ocean theme (`themes/ocean.ts`, art, keys and colours in `themes/ocean/`) keeps the texture key strings from before themes existed. The HUD and the UI are global art (`art/keys.ts`) and look the same on every course; racers, portraits, items, pickups and enemies come from the course's world skin (`engine/worlds/`), so they look the same on every course of a world; adding a theme never edits `art/keys.ts` or `art/index.ts`. A course whose theme or song the engine doesn't have, or whose legend names art its theme has no tile for, fails `themes/course-assets.test.ts`, and the course a session starts on is checked at boot (it throws naming the course and what is missing). The race song is the course's (`Course.song`).

Best times are kept per course and per level (save v6 `bests[courseId][level]`), and the save remembers the last listed course raced (`lastCourse`). An unlisted course (tests, previews, reachable only by `?course=`) never gets a best, never shows NEW BEST and is never the last course.

## Core loop

Pick a course, pick a racer, then a 3-2-1 countdown and the race. In Reef, swim with momentum, dash for bursts of speed, ride currents and avoid kelp drag; in Thornwood, run and jump, stomp enemies, hit crates from below and don't fall in the pits; in Farside, burn up to speed, coast, swing round planets and stay out of the black holes. In all three, fire items. Finish ahead of three rivals drawn from the rest of the cast. Results show place and time; the best finish on each course and level is saved.

## Controls

| Action                     | Keyboard          | Touch |
| -------------------------- | ----------------- | ----- |
| swim (Reef); run (Thornwood); thrust (Farside) | Arrow keys, WASD  | d-pad |
| jump (Thornwood), held for a higher jump | Up, W | d-pad up |
| fire the selected item, confirm | Space, Z, Enter | A |
| menu up/down               | Up/Down, W/S      | d-pad |
| dash, back                 | X, Shift          | B     |
| previous item              | Q                 |       |
| next item                  | E, Tab            | `⇄`   |

Fire (A on touch, Space on a keyboard) uses the item in the selected slot, the one framed in gold on the HUD, and spends one of its charges. Q and E (or Tab, or `⇄` on touch) move the frame to the previous or next held item; pressing next and fire on one tick fires the newly selected item. The dash is the logic's kick, called dash on screen. It runs off a meter of two charges (the pips beside the item well): a dash spends one, and each comes back 1.5 s later (`powerups/dash-meter.ts`), so a dash is saved for a gap, a latch or a squid rather than pressed all race (in Reef; on foot in Thornwood a dash does nothing to a squid, a latch or an Undertow, which the medium's `kickRules` say, so it is saved for gaps and enemies). Rivals use the same meter. How to Play shows it in pictures, and on the first race and every Easy race a hint prompts the first fire and then the first swap.

On course select up and down move the cursor, fire picks the course and dash goes back to the title. On the select screen the arrows (d-pad) move around the grid, fire races as the highlighted character, and dash goes back (to course select when it is shown, else the title). On How to Play, fire or right turns to the next page and dash or left to the previous one. Left and right stop at the first and last page; only dash on the first page and fire on the last page return to the title (with How to play still selected).

## Rules worth knowing

- Tick order and determinism rules are in the header of `src/logic/world.ts`.
- A race must compute the same on every JavaScript engine, because online play will run it on each player's browser from inputs alone. `hashWorld` (`src/logic/hash.ts`) reduces a world's state to one 32-bit number. It is pinned for the top route in `src/logic/top-route.test.ts`, and smoke asserts one number on Chromium, the phone project and WebKit. Logic may not use `**`, and may use only the exactly specified `Math` members on the allowlist in `tools/biome/no-inexact-math.grit` (such as `floor`, `sqrt` and `imul`); `src/logic/num.ts` has exact replacements for the rest.
- Rivals steer by a distance-to-finish field (`race/flowfield.ts`); placing uses a separate uniform-cost field so places don't flicker.
- The race ends when you finish, 10 s after the third rival finishes (you get TIME UP), or at 5 minutes.
- Rivals swim at par with you (swim force 0.95 to 1.10 of yours), so holding forward keeps you in the race and dashes, items and pearls decide it. Rivals fire items often: on a usable target they fire with a per-tick chance of `itemAggression / reactionTicks` (no minimum delay), and a leader with a Mimic drops it on a chaser. Catch-up scales their swim force by 0.05 per tile of gap, between 0.6x when far ahead of you and 1.25x when far behind (Hard's `DIFFICULTY.hard.rivalCatchUp` in `difficulty-level.ts`). Rivals steer around urchins and jellies they can see coming (`ai/avoid.ts`). They aim attacks at any racer ahead of them, you or another rival, and flash a lantern only at a racer just ahead (`ai/items.ts`). Rival Baron plans through coral on a cheap `ram` flow field, since he breaks it by swimming into it. In a dark zone a rival that can't see (not Selene, no Glow Lantern) wobbles twice as much and replans half as often.
- Difficulty is measured by the `difficulty` Vitest project (`pnpm --filter game-rush-racers test:difficulty`, about 76 s, one file per character per bar so they run in parallel; bots in `race-bots.ts` and `bot-driver.ts`). Hard (`<character>.test.ts`, `<character>-novice.test.ts`, bars in `hard-bars.ts`, 30 seeds): an expert wins at least 17, with close wins at least 16 (both 18 before the surface section, cave and hunting sharks), and its mean finish sits within 0.92-1.08 of par (Sunlit's par is 7920 ticks, 132 s); a novice places 3rd or 4th in at least 18 and finishes within 1.3x the winner in at least 27. The roster is not balanced yet: over seeds 1-100 the Hard expert's win rate runs from Baron 67% to Selene 91%, an open problem recorded in `hard-bars.ts`. Normal (`normal-<character>.test.ts`, 10 seeds): a bot that only swims (no dash, no items) places top 3 in at least 6 (measured 9-10); the expert wins at least 4 more races than it (measured 5-9 more); a novice finishes every race, places top 3 in at least 5 and wins at most 8. Rival items (`rival-items-<character>.test.ts`, Normal, 40 races each): every character's median item uses per race is 4.5-10 (the floor was 5 until the harder course took Selene's to 4.5), and `rival-kinds.test.ts` checks every item kind fires. Rivals take two rolls from a clam (the player one), which is what supplies that many uses. Easy (`easy-*.test.ts`): a toddler bot places top 2 in at least 7 of 9, no rival item lands on the player, and the guide halves the time a kid bot spends stuck. `sanity/bars.test.ts` runs in the unit gate: Rudy and Baron on Hard, seeds 13-17, win at least 3 of 5 each and finish near par. Normal's catch-up starts 2 tiles behind the player (`difficulty-level.ts` says why).
- Easy way-finding (`logic/guide.ts`, knobs in `DIFFICULTY[level].guide`, off on Normal and Hard so Hard is unchanged): a player who holds toward the finish and steers only after hitting a wall used to sit against it for many seconds (once about 13 s at the wall at tile 41,4). `guideFor(world, racer)` gives the neighbour tile that lowers the steer field most (the way on, one of 8 offsets) and `world.guide.stuckTicks`, the ticks the player has pushed without reaching a tile closer to the finish by that field. The steering assist: once the player has pushed 0.5 s without progress while nearly at rest, for the next 90 ticks of pushing the part of the rivals' steer aim that is sideways to the stick is added to it (a slide along the wall toward the way on). It never adds speed (swimming normalises the stick), is skipped for a stick held more than 120 degrees away from the way on, and never touches rivals or bots. The arrow (engine, below) shows after 1.5 s stuck. Measured with the stuck-prone kid (`logic/difficulty/kid-bot.ts`: holds right, tries up or down for as long as it keeps getting closer after 1.5 s without progress by the uniform progress field, alternating, kicks every 8 s, never bursts), 40 seeds per character on Easy, stuck time = ticks past the first second of each spell without a step closer by the steer field: Rudy 14.8 -> 2.6 s per race, Gil 19.0 -> 3.1, Onyx 22.7 -> 4.4, Baron 21.1 -> 1.0; mean place 1.70 -> 1.05, 1.82 -> 1.00, 2.10 -> 1.02, 1.40 -> 1.00; DNFs 0, 3, 5, 0 -> 0. `difficulty/easy-guide.test.ts` keeps a 4-seed version of this.
- Slots and humans: a race has four fixed slots (`player`, `tess`, `squib`, `bo`), and `world.humans` lists the ones a person started in (`['player']` unless `createWorld({ humans })` says otherwise). `stepRace(world, inputs, dt)` drives each human slot from its own entry; `stepWorld` is `stepRace` with `{ player: input }` and refuses a world whose `humans` is not `['player']`. A human slot with no entry on a tick is taken over by the AI for the rest of the race (`racer.takeover`): it swims with its character's rival profile and attacks like a rival, but keeps a human's status lengths, clam roll and protection. The rules that once meant "the player" mean any human slot (`isHuman`, `isRival` in `logic/racers.ts`); rivals measure catch-up against the leading unfinished human (`race/catch-up.ts`). A taken-over racer still gets the human catch-up push (`playerCatchUpMul`) while it trails the leader, since that push goes to every human slot (`modifiers.ts`). With several humans the race ends once no active human is left racing; once every rival, taken-over humans included, is home and at most one active human is still racing, that human gets `DNF_GRACE_TICKS` (600 ticks) before the race ends without them, and `RACE_LIMIT_TICKS` ends it in any case (`race/standings.ts`). Easy is single-player in slot 1. The screen follows one slot, the local racer (`engine/local-racer.ts`, `Runtime.localRacer`): camera, HUD, hints, sounds, effects and results are about it.
- Logic calls only the exactly specified `Math` members the lint allowlist names (no `sin`, `cos`, `atan2`, `hypot`, `exp`, `pow`, `log` or the like): engines may approximate the rest, and a seeded race must replay identically on Chromium and WebKit. Use `src/logic/num.ts`.

## Roster

You race as one of eight characters; the race fills the three rival slots with three of the other seven, drawn by seed from the `roster` random stream (`pickRivals` in `logic/characters.ts`). Characters are data in fixed slots (`player`, `tess`, `squib`, `bo`), so tick order, random streams and tie-breaks never depend on who is picked. Abilities live in `logic/abilities.ts` (numbers in `ABILITY`), called from hooks in the systems they change. Each character also has its own rival profile in `logic/characters.ts`. Each character has its own art (`art/sprites/racers.ts`, portraits and heads in `art/ui/portraits.ts`).

Dark zones (`Level.dark`) draw a veil (`view/dark-view.ts`, maths in `view/dark-math.ts`) with a light pool around each racer: yours is 64 px and leans ahead as you swim, a Glow Lantern widens it, and urchins and jellies show a faint glint so you can see them coming. Racers who see in the dark (`racerSeesInDark`) get only a light tint. On Easy the player carries a Glow Lantern's light without holding one (`DIFFICULTY.easy.playerLantern`): a wider pool, and the zone stays dark around it. The course has one dark range, columns 628-706: an underwater cave of craggy rock, stalactites and stalagmites (legend glyphs `R`, `T`, `L`), drawn with a cave-mouth backdrop (`BackdropSpec.cave`).

## Worlds

A world (`logic/worlds/<id>.ts`, a `WorldDef`) is a medium, the names of the cast slots and of the shared items, pickups, enemies and hazards, and its courses (each course names its world). The engine draws it through a world skin (`engine/worlds/<id>.ts`: cast art, portraits, heads and ability wording, item, pickup, enemy and glint art, and the words views show). Character ids (`rudy`, `gil`, ...) are slot ids shared by every world, so the save's pick, abilities, rival profiles and the difficulty files don't change per world: picking Gil in Reef puts the cursor on Skip in Thornwood. Item ids and balance are shared too; only names and art change.

### The run medium

`logic/media/run/`, numbers in its `tuning.ts`:

- Left and right run (top speed 100 px/s before modifiers). Up jumps from the ground on a fresh press; holding it jumps higher (about 3.3 tiles held, 1.1 tapped), and letting go early cuts the jump short. A press up to 6 ticks before landing still jumps on landing, and so does one up to 6 ticks after running off an edge. There is no wall jump and no dropping through a branch.
- Dash is the same button and meter as in Reef: a horizontal burst along facing (200 px/s) that never changes vertical speed, usable once in the air until you land. A jump never spends a dash charge. While you keep steering the same way, a dash's speed over running pace glides back at `dashDrag` (120 px/s², on the ground or in the air), so a dash carries about 0.8 s and gains about 40 px, worth roughly what a swim kick is over cruise. Only the dash glides: mud, a gust that drops and a hit slow you at full friction, letting go of the stick stops you at full friction, and a wall stops you dead.
- Landing on an enemy from above stomps it (`enemy.bonked` with `how: 'stomp'`) and bounces you up; meeting it from the side or from below is a hit, unless you're dashing, as in Reef.
- A shut `?` crate is solid: you stand on it, and jumping into it from below opens it for you. A full inventory leaves it shut.
- A branch (`=`) holds you from above and lets you jump up through it from below; you can't drop through it.
- Pits are open at the bottom. Falling out of the level (`racer.fell`) takes you out for 1 s, then you come back on the last solid ground you stood on with both feet (never a crate), backed off up to 2 tiles from the edge you fell from as far as the ground allows so there is room to jump again, with 1 s of invulnerability (`racer.respawned`). The lost time is the penalty.
- Wind gusts push you along, updrafts and downdrafts push you up and down, and mud slows your running to 0.6x.
- A run level can't have a sea surface (`LevelDef.surface`): open ground is already air on foot, so `validateLevel` rejects any `air` tile.
- Racers push each other sideways only: landing on another racer passes through it, while side bumps, Shade's ambush and Duke's bump mass still work.
- Rivals plan on a jump-link graph (`links.ts`, `nav.ts`): walks, drops and jumps between stand tiles, solved for the least time to the finish, so they take a high route when it is quicker. In the air a rival checks where it would come down at its speed: links are planned at a little under running pace, so one that took off faster (out of a dash, or at a quicker pace) steers back at once when that spot is a pit or a wall, and flies on when it is ground past the tile it aimed for. Racers block each other sideways, so a runner closing on a slower one just ahead on flat ground hops over it when the ground is flat out to where the hop, at its speed, comes down. A loose rival (high wobble) now and then lets go of the stick on flat ground, which is how loose steering costs time on foot. The Easy steering assist only ever adds sideways input, so it never jumps for you; the Easy guide arrow points up at a jump.

### The drift medium

`logic/media/drift/`, numbers in its `tuning.ts`:

- There is no up or down and almost no friction. Holding a direction burns the thrusters that way (260 px/s²) up to a cruise of 115 px/s; letting go leaves you coasting at nearly the speed you had (thin dust takes 16 px/s², plus a small idle brake, larger on Easy). To stop or turn you burn against your motion. A burn can turn or slow a racer above cruise but never adds speed past it.
- Dash is the same button and meter as in Reef: 90 px/s along the stick on both axes, along facing with the stick idle. Speed over cruise bleeds off at `overDrag`, so a dash lasts a second or two.
- Walls bounce: hitting rock returns half the speed into it (`racer.bounced` for an impact of 40 px/s or more). The level is walled all round and has no pits.
- Supply pods open on touch, enemies can't be stomped (a dash bonks them, as in Reef), nebula dust cuts cruise to 0.6x and drags, and solar wind pushes along its arrow and can carry you past cruise.
- Gravity wells (`LevelDef.wells`, `level/wells.ts`) are planets and black holes. Each pulls every racer inside its reach toward its centre, strongest at the centre and fading to nothing at the reach (`pull * (1 - d / reach)`), and like wind it can carry a racer past cruise, which is the slingshot: a pass round a planet on the inside of a turn bends your path for free. Easy scales every pull to 0.6x. A trapped racer feels no pull.
- A black hole is a well with a `horizon`. A racer whose centre crosses it is swallowed (`racer.fell`), is out for 1 s, and comes back at rest on the last spot where it was outside every hole's reach, with 1 s of invulnerability (`racer.respawned`). No shield, Comet or item dash saves it.
- Only a medium with `effects.gravityWells` may race a level that has wells; `createWorld` throws `LevelError` for them elsewhere, and for a start inside a black hole's reach. A start that can only reach the finish through a horizon fails as an unreachable finish, since the horizon's tiles are closed to navigation.
- Rivals steer by velocity (`pilot.ts`, `thrust-plan.ts`): they aim down the distance field, look ahead far enough to brake for a corner, and burn only against the part of a well's pull that carries them off their line, so they ride a planet's pull round a turn. Their distance field charges extra for tiles inside a hole's reach, more the deeper, and never enters a horizon (`nav.ts`).

Course creatures you beat with a dash bonk, or in Thornwood also by landing on them (`logic/hazards/enemies/`, numbers in its `tuning.ts`). Below are Reef's names; Thornwood's beetle, boar and snake behave the same. Touching an enemy in the 12 ticks after a dash, or while boosting, cannonballing or inflated, bonks it; touching it otherwise is a hit. Beaten enemies stay gone and burst into loose pearls. Glyphs: `S` shark, `E` eel, `K` crab.

| Enemy | Does | Beat it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shark | patrols its lane; sees a racer ahead, winds up for 0.5 s (flash and `!`), charges, then rests. Per level (`DIFFICULTY[level].shark`): Easy sees 6 tiles, patrols 3 and rests 40 ticks, charging straight; Normal and Hard see 8, patrol 4, rest 24 and turn after their target mid-charge | two bonks (each staggers it), or one Homing Squid; bursts 5 pearls |
| Eel | hides in a wall hole; eyes and bubbles warn for 0.6 s, then it lunges 2 tiles out and back every 2.5 s. Each eel starts at a point in its cycle set by its column and row (`startEel`), so neighbours lunge out of step | one bonk while it is out; 2 pearls |
| Crab | walks the floor, turning at walls and drops | one bonk; 1 pearl |

On Easy a shark never winds up on the player. Rivals steer around enemies, keep out of a winding-up or charging shark's path, and dash into a crab, an out eel or a charging shark about to reach them when they have a charge. Sunlit Shallows has 3 sharks, 17 eels and 4 crabs after column 60, the eels at chokepoints (the course file lists them).

## Power-ups

Clams (`?` in the course) open on touch, roll an item weighted by place (leaders get defensive kinds, Glow Lantern and Mimic; trailers get speed and the attack kinds; Undertow only rolls in 3rd or 4th, Cannonball mostly there) and stay shut for 1.5 s. You have three item slots (Nib four), never two of a kind. A new item goes into the first empty slot with its charges, and items never slide between slots. With every slot full you don't open a clam: it stays ready for the next racer that touches it, and you don't grab loose items either. Fire uses only the selected item and spends one charge, then item use cools down for 20 ticks. An item's last charge empties its slot and the selection moves to the next held slot to the right (wrapping). There are no combos: each item does one thing.

An item's passive works only while its slot is selected, except that a Bubble Shield in any slot absorbs a hit.

| Jet Fins | 3 | +15% speed, faster kick | boost: 0.4 s at 200 px/s along your input |
| Bubble Shield | 1 | absorbs one hit from any slot | fires a bubble that traps a rival |
| Puffer | 2 | doubles bump push | inflate for 1 s: break coral, hit racers, urchin-proof (never shortens a longer inflation) |
| Ink Sac | 3 | | one ink cloud that slows racers behind |
| Glow Lantern | 2 | pulls in pearls within 3 tiles, lights the dark | flash that stuns nearby racers and jellies |
| Cannonball | 1 | | a 1.25 s dash at 250 px/s, inflated and invulnerable the whole way: smashes coral gates and hits racers it touches |
| Homing Squid | 1 (3 in 3rd or 4th) | a squid that locks the nearest racer ahead within 10 tiles and chases it; on contact it wraps them (70% swim force for 1.5 s, three kicks shake it off in Reef; in Thornwood dashes don't shake it, so the latch runs its time). | Riptide | 2 | every current pushes you 1.5x harder | lays an 8-tile current lane along your aim (snapped to the main axis) that pushes anyone in it at 2x a plain current for 3 s |
| Undertow | 1 | | pulls every racer ahead of you back for 1.5 s, harder the bigger their lead; in Reef their kicks fight it, in Thornwood dashes don't |
| Mimic | 1 | squids lock on you only from half the usual range | a see-through copy of you swims on along the course for 5 s; a squid that picks it pops it instead of you |

Jet Fins and Cannonball do nothing while a dash is already running (no charge is spent). A hit ends a Jet Fins boost; nothing ends a Cannonball early.

A hit (urchin `*`, jelly `j`, bubble, puffer, flash) pops a Bubble Shield from any slot if you hold one, else knocks the selected item and all its charges off as a loose pickup anyone with room can grab after half a second (they get those charges); it floats up in water and sinks on land, stopping at the first solid tile, crate or (from above) ledge, so in Thornwood it rests on whatever the racer stood on. With nothing held you're stunned. The selection then moves on as for a used-up item. Every hit gives a second of invulnerability. A stunned, trapped or latched racer doesn't collide with other racers: you swim straight through it and take the place, and the pair keeps passing through until it separates (`race/separate.ts`, `hazards/contacts.ts`). A stunned or trapped racer can still move the selection, but can't fire, dash or swim. Pearls (`o`) work like coins: each adds 1% to your swim force, up to 10, and gives a short boost; the HUD counts them and a chime plays. A hit that lands (not one a shield absorbs) knocks up to three loose, scattered behind you; anyone can grab them at once, you after half a second, and they fade after 10 s (`pickups/pearls.ts`). Rivals fire one slot at a time (`ai/items.ts`): a Puffer or Cannonball first when coral is just ahead, else the first held slot whose kind has a use (a squid when a racer ahead is in lock range, a Riptide when the water ahead is open and a racer is close behind, Undertow when they trail, Mimic when a squid is chasing them, Jet Fins or a Cannonball on a clear path, but rarely).

## Scenes and launch parameters

- `boot`: builds every texture (the global art in `engine/art/index.ts`, the UI kit and every theme's own), then routes.
- `title`: the logo over the swimming cast and the backdrop of the last listed course raced (Sunlit Shallows until then), then five buttons: Race, How to play, Level (EASY, NORMAL or HARD with 1-3 pips, cycling on each press and saved; a new save starts on Easy and every race is built on the saved level), Sound (mute) and Motion (reduced motion), PRESS A on touch screens only, and your best time and place on the current course at the selected level beside the buttons when it has one (it changes as Level cycles). Settings save at once and apply for the session even if the save fails; a fresh save follows the OS reduced-motion preference. What each level changes is in `logic/difficulty-level.ts`.
- `howToPlay`: seven picture pages (swim, and dash on the meter of two, whose pips empty and refill; open clams, hold three, each with its charge digit; fire one item at a time, drawn as the HUD well with the selected slot framed beside the fire control, a trail from that slot to its effect, then the frame moving on; grab pearls, which count up and add speed, and a hit scatters three; dash to bonk enemies: the crab walks and takes one bonk, the shark shows `!`, charges and takes two, the eel lunges from its hole and is bonked while out; the four attack power-ups; dashing to dodge a squid, or on foot a shield stopping one) with a heading, page dots and a NEXT / BACK footer; each page turn emits `howToPlay.page` (1-based) and plays the menu-move sound. It draws the real sprites, animated unless reduced motion is on, the player's saved racer, key caps on a keyboard and the round A and B buttons on touch. Pages live in `engine/view/how-to-play/`, the control glyphs in `view/control-glyphs.ts`, the paging in `engine/how-to-play.ts` and the page number on the runtime. Page 1 and the crates, enemies and dodge pages belong to the course's medium (`MEDIUM_PAGES` in `how-to-play/index.ts`; a medium without its own uses the shared one): in Thornwood they are RUN AND JUMP (a tap hops, a hold jumps high, then the dash), HIT CRATES (a jump into a crate from below), JUMP TO STOMP (landing on a beetle) and SHIELD STOPS THE BEE (a bee pops a held Sap Shield instead of its racer, since a dash on foot dodges nothing), and the headings, captions and art use the world's names. The backdrop is the current course's level in its theme. Layout in `UI.md` "How to Play".
- `course`: course select, one row per listed course (Sunlit Shallows, Bramble Run, then Slingshot Alley) with its name and your best there on the level in force; the cursor starts on the current course, fire picks it and goes to `select`, dash goes back, and like menus it ignores fire for its first 20 ticks. It is skipped while only one course is listed, and when the course in force is an unlisted one from `?course=` (such as `run-test`): Title Race then goes straight to `select` and the race keeps that course. It is drawn by `view/course-view.ts` and holds five courses; layout in `UI.md` "Course select".
- `select`: the eight characters of the picked course's world in a 4x2 grid (portrait and short name in that world's cast, the cursor's cell in a gold frame) and a card with the highlighted character's full name, ability and what it does. The cursor starts on your last pick. Fire saves the pick and races; dash goes back. Like menus, it ignores fire for its first 20 ticks. Under the card the highlighted racer swims at 2x, and a footer shows the controls (icons on desktop, A/B letters on touch). Layout in `UI.md` "Select".
- Hints for young players (`view/hints.ts`, drawn by `view/hint-view.ts`): on the first race of a save and on every Easy race, PRESS [fire]! (the Space key cap, or A on touch) shows beside the player when an item first lands (until they fire one), [dash] DASH! shows when a locked-on squid is inside the dodge window (`dodgeTicks(world)`: 12 ticks on Hard, 18 on Normal, 24 on Easy) plus a short lead and the player's dash is ready, on a course whose medium lets a dash dodge (`kickRules.dodgesSquid`; never in Thornwood) (PRESS FIRE has no timeout on Easy), SWAP! with the next-item control (E, or `⇄` on touch) shows once a race the first time the player holds two items, until they swap or it has been on screen 4 s (a squid's DASH! pauses it), and the fire glyph pulses beside the HUD's pearl counter once items have sat unused for 5 s. Only one hint shows at a time (DASH!, then PRESS FIRE or the arrow, then SWAP!, then the pulse). On Easy a fifth hint, the guide arrow (`view/guide-view.ts`), points the way on beside the player once they have been stuck 1.5 s; DASH! always outranks it and PRESS FIRE does for the first 4.5 s of being stuck. On touch screens the A button also shows the selected item's icon (`view/fire-button.ts`). `debugSummary()` reports `difficulty` (the setting), `raceDifficulty` (what the race was built on) and `hints` during a race (`hints.guide` is the arrow's tile offset, or null).
- `race`: 3-2-1-GO countdown (the level you are racing on sits under the numeral: its word and 1-3 lit pips, in `debugSummary().levelBanner`), then the race, with FINAL STRETCH and FINISH splashes, or TIME UP if you run out of time. HUD (laid out in `UI.md`; the item well has one slot per inventory place, so four for Nib): place with your portrait (hidden during the countdown; pops up or down on a change), timer, a progress strip with each racer's head, and a well with the dash icon (dim while it recharges) and the item slots: each held item's icon with its charges as one digit, the selected slot in a 1 px gold frame, empty slots a dot. The camera follows you with a deadzone and lookahead. 90 ticks after the race ends you go to results.
- `results`: a podium with medals for the top three, standings with names and times revealed row by row (TIME UP if not finished), your row in sun with your head beside it, a NEW BEST (for this course and level) or BEST NOT SAVED readout, then three buttons (Race again, Character, Title), over the course's own backdrop. While course select is shown, the course's name sits top left.
- Menus ignore fire for their first 20 ticks, so a fire still being pressed from the race can't skip the results.
- Title Race goes to `course` (or `select` while one course is listed, or while an unlisted course from `?course=`, such as `run-test`, is in force); results' Race again races the same course and character again without either select screen (a course picked on course select counts only once select races it: backing out of the selects keeps the course in force before them), while results' Character opens `course` (or `select`, on the same terms) with the cursor on the current pick, so the next race can run as someone else. B on those screens goes back to results with Character selected when they were opened from results, and to the title when opened from Title.
- `?scene=race` (or `?scene=play`) skips the title and races as the saved pick (Rudy on a fresh save). `?seed=7` changes rival behaviour and which characters you race. Each race is seeded with `seed + races in the save`, so a rematch plays differently while a test run still repeats exactly.
- `?course=<id>` races that course, listed or not (`debugSummary().course` and `.medium` say which): `sunlit-shallows`, `bramble-run`, `slingshot-alley`, or the unlisted `run-test` and `drift-test`. A listed one is where the course select cursor starts; an unlisted one skips course select and keeps its course. An unknown id logs one warning naming it and races the default (Sunlit Shallows). Without it the session races the save's last listed course, else the default.
- Save version 6: the best finish per course and level (`bests['sunlit-shallows'].hard`, each a time and place or null; a v5 save's bests move under Sunlit Shallows, and a key for a course the game doesn't know is kept and never shown), `lastCourse`, races run, settings (sound, motion, and `difficulty`: easy, normal or hard) and `character`, the last pick. A v2 save loads as Rudy; a v3 save loads on Easy, like a new one.
- `?debug=1` draws hitboxes from logic state and shows the tick and your tile.
- `?stage=<name>` (needs `?debug=1`, or the test harness) puts attack power-ups into the race before its first frame, to review their views without waiting for a roll: `icons-a`, `icons-b`, `icons-c`, `cannonball` (a Cannonball three tiles before the first coral gate), `squid-flight`, `squid-lockon`, `squid-lockon-far`, `squid-latched`, `riptide`, `decoy`, `undertow`, `dark`, `guide`, `enemies` (a crab 3 tiles ahead on the floor walking away and a shark about 6 ahead, rivals moved clear), `enemy-modes` (every enemy kind in every mode, still, for art review), `surface` (the player at rest 3 tiles under the first surface, sky straight above) (`engine/debug-stage.ts`). A staged race never saves a result. Without the gate the parameter is ignored.
- `?local=<slot>` (needs `?debug=1`, even in the test harness) races with `<slot>` as the only human: the keyboard drives it, the screen follows it, and the AI swims slot 1. `debugSummary()` then describes that racer and adds `local: '<slot>'` (also for `?local=player`), and a `?stage=` is set up around it. An unknown slot logs one warning naming it and races as slot 1, saved as usual. On an Easy save a slot other than `player` races on Normal, with one warning. A `?local=` race never saves a result or shows NEW BEST, even when it beats the saved best (`src/engine/runtime.test.ts`). The rest is checked in `tests/local-racer.spec.ts`.
- Attack power-up warnings: a squid locked on you shows an edge arrow, a LOCKED ON! caption and a rising ping (at most once per 1.5 s); a latched squid shows DASH! beside you (PRESS B on touch) where dashes shake it (`kickRules.shakesLatch`; not in Thornwood). Details in `UI.md`.

## Engine layout

`engine/game.ts` wires Phaser to the shell. `engine/runtime.ts` is the only place logic ticks (`game.ts` feeds its clock once per Phaser step): it reads input, steps the world or the menu, drains each tick's events once and hands them to audio and to views that listen. Scenes in `engine/scenes/` build views from `engine/view/` and sync them every frame. Screens follow `UI.md` (palette, type scale, layout grid, safe zones, motion). The canvas fills the window at a whole-number zoom (`engine/stage.ts`); a resize that changes the view size rebuilds the screen showing, keeping the runtime's menu cursor, page and race. Text goes only through `art/font.ts` `createLabel`; global textures through `art/keys.ts`, a course's own through its theme (`themeFor(course)`).

## Art and audio

Every sprite is palette-indexed pixel text, global art in `engine/art/sprites/` and a theme's in `engine/themes/<id>/sprites/`, drawn at boot by `art/pixel.ts` (at most 4 colours per frame; each racer's main colour holds 3:1 contrast against every theme's contrast bands, and at least a quarter of every racer frame's pixels do, spread over most of its height, enforced in `sprites.test.ts`; the ocean urchin holds 3:1 against the ocean's bands in `themes/ocean.test.ts`). The UI kit (fonts, panels, icons, portraits, logo) lives in `engine/art/ui/`. Sound is a four-voice chip synth (`audio/synth.ts`, `sequencer.ts`); songs are written in a small text notation (`audio/notation.ts`, songs in `audio/songs/`) and parsed at load, so a malformed song fails tests. The race theme speeds up for the final stretch.

## Effects

`engine/fx/` turns logic events into particles and camera shake; racer squash, invulnerability flicker and the 2x Puffer live in the racer and power-up views. How bubbles rise, debris sinks and ink and droplets drift comes from the course theme's particle physics. Effects run per logic tick from events, use their own cosmetic rng (never `world.rngs`), and never change logic state. Shake only ever moves the camera for the player's own hits, shield pops (lighter) and cannonball impacts, capped at 4 px. Reduced motion turns shake off, holds particles still, swaps the flicker for a steady half alpha, drops squash and the place flash, and shows the lantern ring at full reach.

## Sunlit Shallows

About 132 s for a good player, 712 columns in six sections: the reef (columns 0-94, the opening the power-up scenarios rely on); the current highway (95-246: a current tunnel up top or a kelp bed with clams below, then a floor-current hall); the jelly field (247-364: jellies bobbing high and low in step); the surface (365-460: open sky over columns 371-453, where you breach to reach floating clams and pearl arcs, over a patrolling shark); the urchin gauntlet (461-562: an urchin-lined tube or clean roads over and under, with a clam pair at its exit); and the coral gate and the cave (563-711: smash the 2-tile gate with a Puffer or climb over it, pass the gate's shark, then thread the dark cave's irregular urchins, stalactites and eels to the finish at column 707).

The surface (`LevelDef.surface`, `level/surface.ts`) turns the water above its row to air. A racer swimming up through it fast enough breaches: in the air gravity pulls it down at 450 px/s² with light drag and only a little steering (`airGravity`, `airDrag`, `airSteer` in `media/swim/tuning.ts`), a dash or Jet Fins still push, and it splashes back in. `racer.breached` and `racer.splashed` fire on the change. Enemies, loose power-ups and drifting pearls stay in the water.

## Bramble Run

Thornwood's course (`logic/courses/bramble-run.ts`), 490 columns with par 4119 ticks (about 69 s, against Sunlit Shallows' 132 s), in three zones left to right, then a finish straight:

- The forest floor: pits, mud, thorn bushes, beetles and boars, and crates to hit from below.
- The treetop high route: a rock-step climb up a trunk to a branch path of ledges above the floor path, quicker but harder, with gaps, wasps and wind gusts; the floor path below has mud, a headwind, a downdraft and a trench.
- The root cave: a dark zone with snakes and cracked stone to break or jump.

Every enemy kind and cell the run medium supports appears on it at least once. It passes the same difficulty bars as Sunlit Shallows (`logic/difficulty/bramble-run-*.test.ts`), with its par measured as `COURSES.md` "parTicks" says. Its Normal rival catch-up starts 9 tiles behind the player rather than 2 (`catchUp` in the course file): on foot rivals gain less from Normal's force multiplier, and at 2 the no-dash bot fell out of the top 3 too often.

## Slingshot Alley

Farside's course (`logic/courses/slingshot-alley.ts`), 580 columns with par 3551 ticks (about 59 s), an asteroid belt flown left to right:

- The opening straight, to learn the drift, then an S-bend up into the top corridor with a planet at each inner corner to swing round.
- The ice gate: a wall of ice straight ahead for a Comet, a Shock Bubble or Admiral Crater, and a longer way round under an island.
- The dust field, with clean lanes through the nebula, then the solar wind lane.
- A corridor in a moon's shadow, the dark zone.
- The late split round an island. Straight on, a three-tile lane runs between two black holes, inside both reaches, and saves about 1.5 s; the longer way drops round a planet, runs under the island and climbs round another. Rivals take the lane between the holes, and none was swallowed on seeds 1-25.
- The run home to the finish.

It passes the same difficulty bars as the other courses (`logic/difficulty/slingshot-alley-*.test.ts`). Two of its catch-up numbers are its own (`catchUp` in the course file): Normal rivals hover 3 tiles behind the player rather than 2, and the floor of the Easy rivals' multiplier is 0.3 rather than 0.6, because in drift the multiplier takes only half of its cut off cruise, so at 0.6 a rival ahead of a child who stopped still coasted away.

## Course format

ASCII in each course file (`src/logic/courses/<id>.ts`), documented in `src/logic/level/parse.ts`.
